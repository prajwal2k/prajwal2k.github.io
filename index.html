I'm a data engineer who cares less about the tool of the week and more about whether the numbers downstream can be trusted. Most of my work lives in the unglamorous middle of the data lifecycle — ingestion, transformation, storage, and the reporting layer people actually depend on — built to survive schema drift, late data, and the 2am page.

At the University of South Dakota, I automate extraction and transformation across LMS, student, and audit data, and ship the Power BI dashboards leadership uses to make calls — cutting report prep from five days to two hours. Before that, at ITC Infotech, I built and monitored ETL pipelines across enterprise source systems, wired Kafka into backend data flows, and supported a migration into Azure Databricks for large-scale Spark processing.

Alongside the core stack, I've been building applied AI into that same world — RAG pipelines and LangChain tools that let non-technical users query a warehouse in plain English. I'm not trying to be an ML engineer; I want the LLM layer to be one more reliable component sitting on top of pipelines I already trust.
